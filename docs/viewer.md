# The viewer

`python scripts/run_viewer.py` starts it at http://localhost:8501 (`--port` changes it). It only reads the csv files `update_all.py` writes (see [updating-data.md](updating-data.md)); reload the page after an update. On a server it runs in a container: [deploy/README.md](../deploy/README.md).

## Using it

- **League:** the selector at the left of the top row of every page switches between the saves of `config/update.toml`; the choice follows to the other pages. Each league opens on its own `org`, and the first save of the file is the one the viewer opens.
- **Navigation:** a row of links at the top of every page.
- **Values:** the number on most pages is a player's current value (`value_now`): runs above the league's average starter at the position (offense + baserunning + defense add up to it). Ceiling and floor are the 90th and 10th percentile of his best season within the window.
- **Player page:** click a cell of a player's row in any table (the explorer lists, the acquisition tables, the level-moves tables); the depth chart, which is html, has a list of its players under the filters that does the same.
- **Park-adjusted values toggle** (organization, minor leagues, draft, IAFA, depth chart, explorer, acquisitions): values every player as if half his games were played in the park of the organization you are viewing (`home_share` of the save). Home runs, doubles, triples and hits move with the park factors (the left or right one by the side he bats from; singles are what is left of the hits) at the league's run weights, a pitcher's runs allowed with the park's run factor. Fielding and baserunning stay, the chances of being a top-10 or top-50 player are not moved, and a `Park` column shows what the park changes. On the acquisitions page every team's starters are valued in their own park and the candidates in yours. The toggle is off for all organizations and free agents and for leagues without park factors or park models. The hitters' park rates come from `predict_park.py` (the `park` step of `update_all.py`, `viewer/parks.py`).

## The screens

### Organization overview
One dense screen for an organization (the selector also has all organizations and the free agents): the best 20 position players, the best 10 at each position, the best 20 starting and 20 relief pitchers, by current value. Its only choice is the organization.

### Minor leagues
The same layout for players not on the MLB roster, with a Ceiling / Floor switch instead of the current value: the best 90th (or 10th) percentile value within 5 seasons (or the career) and the best chance of becoming a top-10 (or top-50) player at the position.

### MLB draft and IAFA
The same layout for the draft class and the international amateurs. They belong to no organization yet, so there is no selector.

### Depth chart
An OOTP-style field for one organization: the best five at each fielding position and at designated hitter, and the best ten starting and relief pitchers. It is built the way a manager would: the best lineup first, with every player at the position where he adds the most given the others (an assignment problem that maximizes the total value, so the second-best center fielder who is better at right field than anyone else plays right field), then the best backups from the players left; pitchers likewise; each player once.

- **Players: As planned (the default) or As is.** *As planned* draws the plan of the Level moves page: each player at the level the plan puts him (the level shown), the starter of every position and the rotation are the plan's (the lineup against the pitcher's side and the ranking do not apply), the backups are the players of the level ordered by their playing value at the positions they can play (the same passes as below). The whole organization shows the starters of each level from the top down; one level shows five deep. **Orange names** play a position other than the one they are trained at (the designated hitter counts: a shortstop prospect at DH is not learning his position); **hover a name** for the reason of his place and where he comes from. The values are the value now at the slot. The rules, the phase of the majors and the weights of the sliders of Level moves apply when they were set in this session, else the defaults of `config/minors.toml` and `config/update.toml`. *As is* is the chart described above, by the players' files. Code: `viewer/org_plan.py` (the plan, shared with Level moves), `depth.planned_chart`.
- **Show:** the entire organization, the MLB roster alone, the minor leagues, or one level / team (AAA, AA, A+, A-, R+, R, the International Complex, ...).
- **Ranked by:** current value, or the ceiling / floor of his best season within 10 years.
- **Lineup** (Overall, vs RHP, vs LHP; current values only): the hitters against right- or left-handed starting pitchers. A hitter's value against a side is his value now with the batting part swapped for his split (`batting_vs_right` / `batting_vs_left` of the predicted values); fielding and running are the same, and the park effect uses the side a switch hitter bats from against that side. The pitchers stay one list.
- **Red names** are players over the age or pro-years limit of their level.
- Limits: `config/saves.toml`, one table per level (`[TGS27.levels."AA"]` with `max_age` and `max_pro_years`; 0 = no limit; the years come from the ratings column `PROY`). Reload the page after editing. Code: `viewer/depth.py`.

### Level moves (minor league management)
The moves of your organization, level by level, under the limits of the levels. For each level of the ladder (top first) four lists, and a table at the top with the roster size and the pitchers of every level now and after the moves.

- **The majors come first, and they get the best players.** The active roster (`ACT` flag of the export) is the top level, and it is filled before anything else: the best players the organization has, by current value, measured against the league's major leaguers of the same kind (pitcher or hitter). A player of AAA takes the spot of a weaker major leaguer when he is clearly better (and at least as good as the floor of the majors, `floor` in `config/minors.toml`, when the roster has spots left). The rest of the organization is planned after that and adapts: **a problem at AAA is solved from below** (a player of AA moves up, and the ripple continues down), never by sending a major leaguer down. A major leaguer is sent down only because the roster limit needs it or a better player takes his spot, and he can go to any minor league level whose limits he meets, the nearer to the majors the better.
- **Option years.** A player has 3 option years. The first time he is sent down in a season uses one (`OPT` in the export counts the years used) and `OY` counts the times he has been optioned this season: once he has been optioned in a season (`OY` of 1 or more) he can go down and up as often as the season lasts, and otherwise he needs an option year left (`OPT` below 3). With `OPT` at 3 and `OY` at 0 he **cannot be sent down without waivers**: he stays on the roster or is DFA'd, and the plan only DFAs a major leaguer for a large gain or when the roster limit leaves no other way. The tables show the options of the major leaguers (OPT/3, times optioned this season) and the reason of a demotion says which option year it uses. A league whose export has no `OPT` column has no option information and everyone can be sent down.
- **The majors are strict, the minors are about prospects.** At the majors the plan counts a player toward a group by what he can defend, not by his listed position: **catchers** (2 needed), **infielders** (5: a value of 0 runs or more at 2B, 3B or SS; a utility man counts once but covers several positions), **outfielders** (4: at LF, CF or RF) and **bat-only hitters** who cover none of these (first basemen and DHs; a player whose bat at 1B or DH beats his best field position by 10 runs counts as a bat, even if he could play a position). The majors carry **at most 3 bat-only hitters** (a first baseman and a DH platoon of two): a fourth takes the place of the weakest bat, not of a fielder the team needs. In the season the majors carry **exactly 13 pitchers and 13 hitters**; the minors keep the one-over-or-under slack. The minor levels still use the listed position (2B, 3B, SS for the infield; a first baseman does not count). The thresholds (0 runs, 10 runs, 3 bats, the penalty) are hand-tuned constants at the top of `viewer/roster.py`. **Handedness:** the bat-only hitters of the majors should not lean to one side (the DH platoon needs a left-handed and a right-handed bat): no more than one more left-handed than right-handed bat, or the reverse, and a switch hitter leans neither way. A switch hitter is also worth a little more (he faces the weaker side of the pitcher; one with a weak side gets the same small bump as any, since the splits are not used). A bat-only hitter among the best of the league's major leaguers (`REGULAR_BAT`, 85%) plays every day and is left out of this balance: he is no platoon player, and the balance never sends the best bat down. All of these are hand-tuned (`SIDE_BALANCE`, `SIDE_PENALTY`, `SWITCH_BONUS`, `REGULAR_BAT`). Not modelled yet: which positions each backup covers one by one, and the platoon by the vL / vR splits.
- **The bottom level trims by release.** The lowest level of the ladder that players are demoted into (not the international complex, not a winter league) has nobody below to take its extra players, so when it is over its maximum the fillers (no prospect with development left) are released, the weakest first, at a low cost (`BOTTOM_RELEASE` in `viewer/roster.py`, hand-tuned). Prospects there are kept as before. **A release never takes a good player when a weaker one can go:** the cost of releasing a player grows with his quality, the larger of where his value now stands among the league's major leaguers and (for a prospect with development left) his ceiling (`QUALITY_RELEASE`, hand-tuned, below the cost of breaking a roster limit). So the veterans past their window and the depth players with the lowest value go first, and the best pitchers of the organization stay; if only good players are left, a level stays over its maximum and the summary shows it in red.
- **No future, no place.** A player whose ceiling stands below 3% of the league's major leaguers of his kind (`ceil_mlb`, from the `Ceil` of the outlook; `NO_FUTURE_CEILING`) is released even when his level is not over its maximum, with the reason `no future: his ceiling stands at N% of the league's major leaguers`. The worst ceilings go first, never more than takes a level below 80% of its maximum (`LEVEL_FLOOR_SHARE`: a level needs players to field a team), and the cover of a level (catchers, infielders, outfielders, starters) and its pitcher target still keep the players they need. Not the majors, not the international complex. The gain of a release (`NO_FUTURE_GAIN`) and the other numbers are hand-tuned. Teenagers are included: a 17 year old with such a ceiling is released too.
- **Injured players are not moved.** A player the export shows as injured (`INJ` Yes, with the time left in `Left`) stays where he is until an export says he is well: he is not promoted, demoted or released, he keeps his place against the roster limits, and he does not count as cover for a position. When the ratings would have moved him, the reason says `injured (12 months): not moved until he is well`. There is no cutoff in days: a short injury holds the move until the next export. A league whose export has no `INJ` column holds nobody.
- **The roster phase.** Choose it on the page: Season and playoffs (26), Offseason (40) or Spring training (60); the page starts at the league's `phase` in `config/update.toml` (`season`, `offseason` or `spring`; change it there as the game moves on), or guesses from the typical team's active count when none is set, and updates when you switch. **Choose 26 in spring training to simulate the cutdown** for the start of the season: the 40-man players are all on the active roster in spring training, so the plan shows who is demoted (those with an option left), who has to stay (no option left, unless the gain from a replacement is large) and who is DFA'd. In the season the majors want **13 pitchers: 5 starters and 8 relievers** (`[mlb]` in `config/minors.toml`; a starter in the bullpen is the long reliever, so a starter is never flagged as a reliever there; a reliever among the 5 best starters is flagged SP). There is no pitcher target in the offseason or spring training. A league whose export has no `ACT` for the majors shows only the minor levels.
- **Every level must be able to field a team** (the majors too): at least **2 catchers, 5 infielders** (2B, 3B, SS by the listed position; **a first baseman does not count**: he covers first base only, so a backup who can play the middle infield is not sent down for a third first baseman), **4 outfielders** (LF, CF, RF and a utility man) and the players of its **rotation who can start**: 6 at every minor league level, 5 in the majors (the listed starters and the relievers who rank as starters count). The plan keeps this after every move: a catcher is not sent down when he is the second one, a level short of a group gets one from the level next to it (the majors can pull a player up for it), and the weakest spare is the one who is cut or moved. The summary table shows what each level has after the plan in **Catchers, Infielders, Outfielders and Can start**, in red when it is short (when no player is available to fix it). The international complex, where players are placed automatically, is not checked.
- **Promote / Demote:** from the ratings. The key ratings (hitters: contact, gap, power, eye, avoid K's, BABIP; pitchers: stuff, movement, control) are compared with all players of the level in the league, the way you read the game's ratings relative to a level; statistics are not used. Up: green in enough key ratings and not at the bottom of the next level, or old for the level with average ratings (no stagnating), or over the age or pro-years limit of the level. Down: weak for the level.
- **DFA or release:** a player no level can take (too old for the level below, which has a lower age limit, and the levels around are full). The one with the least reason to be kept goes first; prospects with development left stay. **DFA** when he is on the 40-man roster (the `ON40` flag), **Release** when he is not; a league without the flag says "DFA / Release".
- **Listed at a position that is not their best:** a hitter whom the best lineup of his level (one per position, the most total value, as the depth chart builds it) plays at another position, with the runs he would add there (flagged from `position_gap`, 2 runs). Bench players have no position to get wrong. For pitchers: a level wants **6 starting pitchers** (`starters`); a reliever who is among the 6 best starters of his level (by his value as a starter) is flagged "SP", and a starter who is not "RP". The table at the top shows the listed starters and the starters after these changes.
- **The limits are respected.** A move is only to the next level up or down, only to a level whose age and pro-years limits the player meets (a 26-year-old does not go down to a level with a maximum age of 25), and a player over the limits of his level cannot stay. A level never holds more than its `max_players`. A level with `no_demotion = true` (the international complex, where the signed 16 and 17 year olds are placed automatically) is never a destination of a demotion; its players can be promoted out of it. The winter leagues are off the ladder (`ladder = false`) and not managed here yet. A level that is too full sends its fillers down (the weakest first), not up, and never sends one to the majors to make room.
- **Pitchers are a target, not a maximum.** Pitchers are hurt more often than hitters, so every level should have `pitchers` of them, one more or one fewer being fine (`pitcher_slack`). A level with too few gets pitchers from the levels next to it, one with too many sends its extra ones to a level that is short, and when a level has to shed players the cut comes from the side that has too many (a player you want to keep or move is not released for the sake of the count). A move made only for the count costs what the player is worth keeping, so prospects are not moved for it. If no pitcher is available the level stays short; the table at the top shows it in orange.
- **What a level is for.** The top level (AAA, no age limit) is the last stop for prospects before the majors and the place for players close to the majors, as cover for injuries there. So at the top level a player is worth keeping by how close his ratings are to the majors (how they stand at the level above) as well as by his ceiling; the lower the level, the less that counts and the more prospect status (the ceiling, with development left) counts. At the bottom a player without prospect status is only filler: he is demoted or released first.
- **Who plays.** The plan decides the levels and who plays where together; the lineups, the bench, the rotation and the bullpen are on the **depth chart** (Players: As planned), each player with the words for his place on hover. This page keeps the summary above the levels, which counts, per level, the players placed, the starters **off their primary position**, the ones the depth chart (the most current value per position) would place elsewhere, and the sum of the playing values. The value of a place has four parts (`viewer/playing.py`, settings `[playing]` in `config/minors.toml`, sliders on the page; the weights are hand tuned guesses):
  - **Dev** (development): his ceiling x the years of development window left x how young he is for the level (older than the level's median counts less) x the share of the season he plays at the slot (a starter all of it, the bench 15%, a reliever 30%) x his **growth** (below).
  - **Learn**: the rating he builds at the position by playing it, `3.3` points per 100 innings in the rookie leagues, `1.5` in A ball, `0.6` at AAA and AA and `0.08` in the majors (measured in the weekly runs, docs/models.md), at most up to his potential at the position; it counts by his ceiling and not at all once his window is over. **The position he is trained at comes first**: learning there counts more (twice as much at C, SS and CF, where a 70 or better is a long climb), and learning at another position counts in full only once his own is close to its potential (80% of the way from 20, 90% at the key positions), a fifth of it before.
  - **Hit**: what a position he is still learning costs in performance (his hitting suffers while he learns it, less as he does), subtracted: 30% of his development value times how far he is from his potential there.
  - **Now**: where he stands among the league's players at the slot, a floor so that a level can still field a team.
  A name in orange on the depth chart plays a position other than the one he is trained at. **Grow** (the growth, used in the value of a place) is the change of his ratings over the last year in 20-80 points (his potential counts too), from the archive of exports (`model/growth.py`, step `growth` of `update_all.py`): with talent change randomness on, a point gained last year is about 0.8 more over the next three for a hitter (0.4 for a pitcher), so a player who is rising counts for more. It is empty until the archive has two exports 90 days apart, and is relative to the league (a rebalance of the ratings is not growth). The weight of who plays in the choice of the levels (`joint_weight`, 0 chooses the levels as before) is the first slider of the page. The majors are filled first, as before; their lineup comes from the same values, where the current value counts most.
- **Focus:** the better players while they can still develop. A prospect has a ceiling in the better fifth of the league's minor leaguers of his kind and a development window (what is left until the age where ratings stop growing: hitters 25, pitchers 26, set per game version). Prospects are the last to be demoted or released, and their moves come first in the lists.
- Rules: `config/minors.toml` (changeable on the page for the session). Per level in `config/saves.toml`: `max_age`, `max_pro_years`, **`max_players`**, **`pitchers`** (0 = no limit / no target) and `no_demotion`, and the promotion ladder (the order of the level tables, top first; `ladder = false` leaves a level out). Code: `viewer/minors.py` (the moves of the ratings), `viewer/roster.py` (the plan under the limits).

### Acquisitions (MLB roster)
Where the starting lineup, rotation and bullpen of your organization are weak against the starters of the league, and who could upgrade it.

- **Needs:** the best lineup of the MLB players is chosen (one per position), five starters and `bullpen` relievers; each starter is placed among the league's starters at his place. A place nobody can play (a missing catcher) is the biggest need.
- **Candidates:** your own players who are not starters (internal: the bench and the minor leaguers), players on waivers (claimed with their contract) and free agents (their one-year demand, signability shown). A demand of 0 is a minor league deal.
- **Cost** is against the budget space of the season (from the `finance` folder of the export, `<save>-<season>-finances.csv`); without it the costs are not checked.
- **Priority list** (the top table): the acquisitions in the order to act.
  - *Impact* is the runs a player adds over his contract (three years at most) against the starter and against what your own prospects at the place will be worth in one and two years. A year in which he would keep a ready prospect out of the lineup counts against him. *Per yr* is Impact divided by the years.
  - *Expected* is Impact times the chance he signs (by signability: the demand can change before he does) times a popularity boost. Local popularity counts most (the local market, fan interest, which matters when yours is low): +10% for a very popular (V.Pop) and +20% for an extremely popular (Ex.Pop) player. National popularity (awards, all-star votes) counts little: +2% and +4%; the two add up. Columns *Local pop* and *Nat. pop*. It is a tiebreaker, since it scales an impact that is small for a player who adds little. The list is sorted by Expected. *Fallbacks* are the next options at the place.
  - *Verdict:* **Hole** = nothing of your own reaches the league target within two years, so a long deal is fine; **Bridge** / **Soon** = a prospect reaches it in one / two years, so prefer a short deal; **Fill** = the place is no need. A free agent asking `multiyear_demand` or more ($8m) is assumed to want three years.
  - *Spend:* the list never takes the budget space under `min_budget` ($10m, kept for the draft, IAFA and other areas). **Within** fits, **Over** would take it under the minimum after the deals above it.
  - A good player can cover two places as a backup, so one of your players may be counted as the future answer at more than one.
- **Best set of acquisitions:** the largest total gain that fits the budget space (an integer program: one acquisition per place, each player once), and a table of candidates for any place.
- Rules: `config/acquisition.toml` (changeable on the page). The export columns it reads: `WAIV` (on waivers), `DEM` (demand), `Sign` (signability), `SLR` and `YL` (contract). Code: `viewer/acquire.py`.

### Rule 5
Prepares the draft. Your draft-eligible players (the `R5` flag of the export) with a recommendation:

- **Protect** (on the 40-man) when he is a prospect with development left whose ceiling would start for your MLB team (or a top prospect) and he is ready for the majors, when he is ready and would start now, or when his potential is high (ceiling in the better tenth of the league's minor leaguers at his position: a rebuilding team drafts on potential and does not mind the roster spot).
- **Low risk** when he is a prospect who is not ready, so unlikely to be picked. **Expose** for the rest.
- **40-man arithmetic:** on it today (the `ON40` flag; a league without it gets an estimate, marked on the page), open slots, the players you protect, the free agents you plan to sign, how many have to come off.
- **Who could come off:** the players with the least to keep them (not starters, no prospects, lowest value), with their contracts.
- **Draft candidates:** draft-eligible players of other organizations who would improve your MLB roster, and a last table with the exposed prospects of other organizations with that much potential whether or not they help now (for a weak season).
- Rules: `config/rule5.toml`. Code: `viewer/rule5.py`.

### Explorer
A list builder with many options (list, seasons ahead, sort, players from, positions, age, levels, organization) and an Excel report with the ordered list, a block per position and the filters used. Code: `viewer/queries.py`, `viewer/overview.py`, `viewer/report.py`; the lists are defined in `config/viewer.toml` (also `outlook_lists.py`).
