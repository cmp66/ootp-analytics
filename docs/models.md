# Models and outlook tables

Reference for the value models, the outlook models and the leagues with other rating scales. For the steps to retrain them see [workflows.md](workflows.md), workflows 2b, 3 and 4.

## Models

The value models predict, from a player's ratings, his batting, baserunning, pitching and fielding value (runs per 600 PA, per 200 IP or per season). They are trained, evaluated and applied from scripts; the notebooks and the `Hitting/Pitching/Fielding/BSRModel` classes are the old code and stay until `DevModels` and `reports/PredictionMaker.py` are ported.

```
python scripts/train_models.py Model-Sim --start 2046 --stop 2086      # train every model of a save
python scripts/evaluate_models.py Model-Sim --calibration              # scores stored at training, scores on seasons
python scripts/predict_values.py Model-Sim --season 2086               # predicted values of one season to a csv
python scripts/predict_values.py TGS27 --models-from Model-Sim           # models trained on one save score another
```

- **Training data** is the processed output of a save (`scripts/process_seasons.py`); the ratings set in `config/saves.toml` names the model folder `files/models/<save>/<ratings>/`, one `.joblib` file per model (weights, scaler, feature names, seasons, scores, platoon mix).
- **Models:** batting, pitching FIP part and pitching BABIP part each have a stacked platoon model (`<stream>-split`: value against right- and left-handed opponents, blended with the league's platoon mix of the player's hand) and a plain total model as a fallback; baserunning; one fielding model per position (C, 1B to 3B, SS, LF, CF, RF). The attribute lists and the model family per stream are in `model/domains.py` (`REVIEWED_FEATURES`, `CHOSEN_FAMILY`); every listed rating exists in the dump and in the in-game export, so models trained on a dump save can score an export save.
- **Evaluation** is player-grouped: no player is on both sides of a split, the network stops on validation players taken from the training set only, and `--start/--stop` of `evaluate_models.py` can score seasons the models never saw (train with `--stop 2080`, evaluate 2081 to 2086).
- **Hardware:** the models are small; ridge and boosting run on the CPU and the networks train on the CPU by default (`--device cuda` or `OOTP_DEVICE=cuda` uses a GPU where available; the Intel NPU cannot train PyTorch models and is not visible from WSL). Artifacts are pickles: only load files you trained.
- **Output** of `predict_values.py`: `batting`, `batting_vs_right`, `batting_vs_left` (runs per 600 PA), `baserunning`, `pitching_fip*` and `pitching_runs` (runs per 200 IP), `pitching_babip` (BABIP above league), `fielding_<position>` (runs per season if he played there), and the `_plain` fallbacks.
- **League-relative values** (`<column>_rel`): each value column has a twin with the playing-time weighted mean of that column over the scored league and season subtracted (`model/relative.py`). The raw columns are values against the league the models were trained on. A league that picks players differently (for example human managers who play good defenders at SS, CF, 2B and 3B) has a different average, and the raw fielding values then run high for it (shortstops of a human OOTP 26 league, +22 runs); the `_rel` columns are values against the league being scored. The script prints the shift it subtracted for each column. The shift is about zero for the league the models were trained on.

## Leagues with a different rating scale
Some leagues show ratings on the 1-10 scale (PBL27: everything; Dugout26: speed, stamina and fielding, batting and pitching stay 20-80). A 1-10 rating is a coarser bucketing of the same underlying rating, and where the buckets end differs per rating column. `config/rating-scales/<version>.csv` (from two dumps of one league taken at the same moment, one per scale; `scripts/build_scale_tables.py`) holds how often each 20-80 value is each 1-10 value. `scripts/compare_scale_approaches.py` (see `docs/rating-scale-comparison-*.md`) found that retraining the value models on ratings turned to 1-10 loses about 0.015 (OOTP27) to 0.025 (OOTP26) of R^2 on average, and converting 1-10 up to 20-80 and reusing the 20-80 models loses about twice that, so `scripts/train_models.py <training save> --scales <league>` trains a model set for the league's mix (`files/models/<save>/<ratings>@<league>/`) and the `scales` key of `config/saves.toml` names it. A league that plays with relative ratings off (PBL27, `relative_ratings = false`) uses the `<version>-direct.csv` table instead, because its top 1-10 buckets for pitchers mean less (see "Development analysis" below). `scripts/detect_rating_scales.py <league>` shows which columns of a league are on which scale. The outlook models stay on the 20-80 scale: a league with a profile gives them the expected 20-80 value of each 1-10 rating. Workflow: `docs/workflows.md`, 2b.

## Outlook tables (player development)
`python scripts/build_outlook_tables.py Model-Sim` builds, for a long CPU league, the tables the outlook models of `plans/16-development-models.md` are trained on, in `files/<save>/outlook/` (`players.csv.gz`, `values.csv.gz`; about 2 minutes for all seasons). `model/outlook_data.py` documents the columns.
- **players**: one row per player and season: age, level, entry route (international / high school draft / college draft / other) and entry season, work ethic, intelligence, leadership, the four prone fields, playing time at every level and `time_share` (a proxy for missed time), the gate ratings, and `stay_h1/2/3/5` (still in the league that many seasons later; NaN, never False, when that season is beyond the table).
- **values**: one row per player, season and slot (C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, LF, CF, RF, DH, SP, RP): the league-relative value of his ratings at that slot (from the plan 14 models, not from stats or playing time), the rank among the qualified players of the league and the tier (top 10, top 50, rest; the cutoffs scale with the number of teams: 30 teams give ranks 10 and 50).
- Traits come in two forms so that one model can score both file formats: the dump holds numbers (1-200), the in-game export only categories (L / N / H; proneness Iron Man / Durable / Normal / Fragile / Wrecked). `model/formats.py` turns the dump numbers into the export's categories (cut points estimated by matching the export's shares, stored in `config/outlook.toml`; proneness in three levels because Iron Man and Wrecked depend on history), and the export import keeps the categories in `<trait> cat` columns. `python scripts/check_format_parity.py` writes `docs/export-dump-parity.md`, the check of what is in both formats. Adaptability is only in the export. A player is ranked at a position when his key ratings reach the position's minimum, or his potential rating there reaches the potential minimum. The minimums are measured per ratings set (1st percentile of the top-50 MLB starters at the position, floored to a multiple of 5) and kept in `config/outlook.toml`, where they can be edited; `--remeasure-gates` measures them again.

`python scripts/review_outlook.py <save>` (stage 2 of plan 16, about a minute) writes `docs/outlook-diagnostics-<save>.md`: for one game version, how every rating develops with age and what work ethic, intelligence, proneness and missed playing time change (separate low and high tiers; leadership, loyalty and finances as placebos), who stays in the league, the aging of value, the baseline the outlook models must beat, the playing time lost to proneness, and what high school, college and international entrants must reach at entry. The csv tables go to `plans/data/outlook-review/`.

`python scripts/train_outlook_models.py <save>` (stage 3 of plan 16, about 1 hour per game version) trains the outlook models of that version: quantiles of where a player's value is going (floor, median, ceiling), the chance of being a top 10 / top 50 player at a position, of staying in the league and of being available, for 1, 5, 7 and 10 seasons ahead (`TRAINED_HORIZONS` in `model/outlook_models.py`; 2 and 3 were dropped to save training time), plus a career view (the best season within the next 10: its floor, median and ceiling and the chance of ever being top 10 / top 50). Artifacts go to `files/models/<save>/<ratings>/outlook/`; the held-out evaluation to `docs/outlook-model-evaluation-<save>.md`.

`python scripts/predict_outlook.py <save> [--models-from <save>]` (stage 4 of plan 16, about 15 seconds) scores every player of a season with the outlook models of the same game version and writes `<save>-<season>-outlook-slots.csv` and `-outlook-players.csv` next to the other outputs: the floor, median and ceiling of the value and the chance of being a top 10 / top 50 player at each position 1, 5, 7 and 10 seasons ahead (and 2 and 3 in files made before they were dropped) and for the career view (`_h99`), the chance of staying in the league, availability, the injury risk and cost, and a depth flag. It works on dump and export saves (for example `Dugout26 --models-from Model-Sim-26-Std`). Columns: `docs/outlook-columns.md`.

`python scripts/outlook_lists.py <save> --list very_good --slot SS [--org ABBR]` (stage 5 of plan 16) prints the lists of `config/viewer.toml` from the outlook files: the very good players (chance of being a top-10 player at a position), the good ones (11th to 50th), the ordered ranking, the draft class, the IAFA prospects, free agents, trade targets, and how each player compares with your organization's roster (`p_start`, `p_roster`). The queries are pure functions in `viewer/queries.py`, which the viewer of plan 15 will call.

## Generated reports in docs/

- `docs/derived-ratings.md`: how the derived ratings are made (contact `CON` is half `BABIP` and half `AVK`; movement `MOV` is about 80 % home runs allowed and 20 % `PBABIP`), with the fitted weights per save. Regenerate with `PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/derived_ratings.py`.
- `docs/model-attribute-ranking.md`: which ratings contribute most to each model's value, with the effect of +10 points and +1 standard deviation in runs. Regenerate after retraining with `PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/rating_importance.py`.
- `docs/model-attribute-ranking-ootp26.md`: the same ranking for the OOTP 26 models (`PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/rating_importance.py Model-Sim-26-Std --out docs/model-attribute-ranking-ootp26.md --observations docs/model-attribute-observations-Standard26.md`).
- `docs/ootp26-vs-ootp27-attribute-impact.md`: how the effect of each rating changed between the OOTP 26 and OOTP 27 models, ranked by the size of the change, and how the two model sets value the same ratings. Regenerate with `PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/compare_versions.py` (about 5 minutes).


## Human league calibration of pitchers' control, PBABIP and stamina

The models learn from a computer league. Checked against the human leagues (TGS27, PBL27, Dugout26; one season each, about 400 pitchers per league, `scripts/check_human_bias.py`), the pitchers with poor control did worse than predicted and those with good control better, in all three: 2.9, 2.6 and 1.7 runs per 200 IP per standard deviation of the control rating (pooled 2.4, z 5.8; with the other ratings in the same regression). The PBABIP rating (hits on balls in play) and stamina show the same sign in all three, about half as large: pooled +1.1 (z 2.5) and +1.2 (z 2.7). In the computer league the same effects are about zero. Applying round numbers (control 2.0, PBABIP 0.8, stamina 1.0) takes 6.4 % (TGS27), 2.2 % (PBL27) and 5.5 % (Dugout26) off the residual variance, 1.2 to 1.6 points more than control alone; season-to-season noise dominates (a residual sd of 13 to 15 runs per 200 IP), so the corrections matter at the extremes, not for the middle of the pack. An earlier leave-one-league-out test of the control correction (fit on two leagues, apply to the third) explained 5.1 % (TGS27) and 2.9 % (Dugout26) and nothing in PBL27.

`pitching_control_per_sd`, `pitching_pbabip_per_sd` and `pitching_stamina_per_sd` in `config/saves.toml` (2.0, 0.8 and 1.0 for the three human leagues, 0 for the computer leagues) add that many runs per 200 IP for each standard deviation of a pitcher's rating over the league (control and PBABIP: the mean of the vs-right and vs-left ratings; stamina: STM), measured among the pitchers who pitched in the season and capped at +-2.5 standard deviations (`model/relative.py`, `calibrate_pitching`). They go into the FIP values and `pitching_runs`, so `value_now` and the outlook scores of the human leagues include them (the amounts are in the `pitching_control_adjustment`, `pitching_pbabip_adjustment` and `pitching_stamina_adjustment` columns of the predicted values). The outlook models themselves are not retrained: they learn from the computer league's tables, so their ceilings and floors still use the old weights except through the value they start from. Re-run `update_all.py` after changing a number; `check_human_bias.py` shows whether the pooled estimates still hold as more human league seasons arrive. (Movement `MOV` is not a model input: it is derived from home run avoidance and PBABIP.)

What was checked and found **not** to need a correction:

- **Stuff** (inconsistent in sign, pooled +0.5 runs per sd, z 1.2) and **the starter or reliever role** (+2.3, -2.7, +0.9 between leagues: the role pattern of the computer league, where starters fall short and relievers beat the prediction by about 1.5 runs per 200 IP, does not carry over).
- **Home run avoidance.** The home run rate falls by 15 to 20 % of the league rate per standard deviation of the rating in every league, human or computer, so the rating works the same. Its residual in the FIP model is inconsistent between the human leagues (+2.7, +0.4 and -0.5 runs per sd). Parks matter in the human leagues (the effective home run factor of a pitcher's park varies by 5 to 8 % between teams, the computer leagues have neutral parks) and the pitcher home run rate follows them (an elasticity of 0.7 to 0.9 of the factor). Pitchers are adjusted for the run factor only; the leftover is a small credit for pitchers in home-run-friendly parks (about +1.4 runs per 200 IP at a factor of 1.10, not significant).
- **Hitters**: after the league-wide shift, contact, eye and strikeouts are within about one run per 600 PA; power runs slightly high.

## Fine age

The exports and the dumps give a player's age as a whole number of years, but development follows a smooth curve of the exact age: in the training league a player who just turned 24 gains about 4.6 value points over the next season on average and one about to turn 25 about 2.7. The outlook models therefore use `AgeFine` (years and fraction of a year, at the date of the player's ratings) instead of the whole age; the whole age is kept for age bands and baselines. On held-out seasons this lowers the pinball loss of the value models by 0.3 to 0.6 % (hitters and pitchers, one and five years ahead), small because individual seasons are noisy, but consistent.

The date of a file is not in it, so it is found from its players (`util/ages.py`): each player's date of birth and whole age say the date is on or after his birthday of that age and before the next one, and over thousands of players the window closes to one day (TGS27 2046-02-26, PBL27 2081-12-28, Dugout26 2198-07-01; the training dumps are taken on 1 or 15 October). The `Sct` column of an export (the date a player was last scouted) is the date of his ratings: it is the export date in TGS27 and Dugout26, and in PBL27 most players' ratings are from 29 October, 60 days before the export, so the fine age is measured on that date (`RatingDays` in the player data says how old the ratings are). `AgeFine` and `RatingDays` are columns of the processed player data. `scripts/copy_exports.py` keeps every new ratings export, gzipped, under its in-game date (`files/<save>/archive/`), so the changes of ratings between exports can be studied.

## Relative and non-relative ratings, and the rebalance of ratings

OOTP ratings can be **relative** or not. In a relative league (TGS27, Dugout26, and the training leagues Model-Sim and Model-Sim-26-Std) an 80 is high against the other players of the save: the game keeps its target distribution of ratings across the save, so about the same share of players has a 70 or more at home run avoidance in every season. To do that the game **rebalances** the ratings as the players change: typically when the new offseason starts (late October in PBL27, where most players' ratings carry that date in the `Sct` column) and around opening day. A rating can therefore change without the player having changed, and a snapshot just before the offseason rebalance and one just after differ for every player. In a non-relative league (PBL27) a rating is a direct mapping of the underlying 1-600 skill to the bucket (1-10 there), so it does not follow the league as the talent drifts.

What this means for the work so far:

- The training leagues are relative: across 40 to 60 seasons the mean and the 95th percentile of the ratings of the rostered players hardly move (for instance the mean power rating of Model-Sim moves between 25.2 and 25.5, and that of Model-Sim-26-Std between 30.3 and 30.6), as a rebalanced league should. The ratings of TGS27 and Dugout26 are on the same footing, so the models apply to them as they are. PBL27's 1-10 buckets are an absolute scale: the models for it (trained on ratings turned into its mix of scales, `config/rating-scales/`) use the **direct** conversion table (`config/rating-scales/OOTP27-direct.csv`, `relative_ratings = false` in `config/saves.toml`; see the next section), and the calibrations of PBL27 (`pitching_*_per_sd` are per standard deviation of the league, so they do not depend on the scale) are the better-founded part.
- The annual change in the training tables (from October to October) always contains one offseason rebalance, and one around opening day. The training snapshots are all on 1 or 15 October, before the offseason rebalance. A human export is taken on other dates (TGS27 26 February, PBL27 28 December, Dugout26 1 July); the next rebalance is then less than a year away, so how far ahead "one year" is differs. The archive of exports (`files/<save>/archive/`) shows on which dates the ratings of many players move together: the weekly runs of the next section show that development is a steady drift through the year, not a step at the rebalance, so `AgeFine` (the age on the date of the ratings) already puts an export on the right footing and the age is not shifted.

## Development analysis (weekly and biweekly runs of Model-Sim)

`files/Development-Analysis/` holds database dumps of the Model-Sim league run on for one year from 2086-10-21 (OOTP 27), three times from the same starting point: **Relative80** (20-80, relative ratings on, a dump every week, no re-scouting: ratings change when the game updates them), **Relative10** (1-10, relative on) and **Direct10** (1-10, relative off), the last two every two weeks and every player re-scouted just before each dump. The folder names are day and month only; the in-game date of a dump comes from the dates of birth and ages in it (`util/ages.py`). Everything below is one league lineage and one year: strong evidence, not proof.

- **When ratings change.** On an ordinary week 0.3 to 1% of the ratings of the players in a league change. Two weeks stand out: 2086-10-28 (the offseason rebalance, about 2.4% of the ratings, 15% of the players change a rating) and 2087-03-31 (opening day, about 3%). They hold only about 14% of the year's movement. The weekly rate of hitters roughly doubles from the end of February (spring training).
- **The hitter rebalance is small.** Hitters' ratings move by about -0.25 (in 20-80 points, averaged over the ratings) at the October rebalance and +0.2 at opening day, the same at every age, so they cancel over the year; pitchers have none. A +0.25 on all ten hitting ratings is worth 0.67 runs per 600 PA in the batting model (against about 13 runs of spread between regulars), so there is no phase flag for hitters in the models.
- **Development is a steady drift.** Mean change in a rating over the year (20-80 points): hitters +1.2 to +1.7 at 20-21, +1.0 to +1.4 at 22-23, about +0.5 at 24-25, about 0 at 26-27, -0.3 to -0.4 at 28-30 and -0.6 to -1.1 at 31 and over; pitchers peak at 21-24 (about +1) and are flat from 28. Gap fades first, eye lasts longest (still flat at 31+), speed holds until 31 and then falls by about a point. The change accrues evenly from the October dump to the next September, so an export taken in June has already had most of that year's change.
- **Potential is not fixed.** The `talent` (potential) ratings of hitters fall with age: by 28-30 they have lost one to two points. Young players more than 2 points below potential grow about twice as fast as those at it.
- **Relative against direct 1-10.** At the same moment 95% of the rating cells are identical. The relative setting lowers pitchers' PBABIP (22% of the cells differ, -0.22 on the 1-10 scale), control (-0.09) and stuff (-0.05), and raises hitters' strikeouts (+0.09) and contact (+0.04); speed, stamina and fielding are identical. The gap is constant through the year: the relative setting adds no drift of its own, and the development by age is the same.
- **The conversion for PBL27.** `OOTP27.csv` matches the relative 1-10 within 0.3 20-80 points. With relative ratings off the top buckets of pitchers are worth less on the 20-80 scale (a control of 8, 9 and 10 is 59.7, 65.7 and 72.5 where the relative table says 64.7, 70.9 and 76.9). PBL27 plays with relative ratings off (its MLB pitchers look like Direct10, not Relative10: PBABIP 5.81 against 5.76 and 5.53), so it uses `OOTP27-direct.csv`, built from the first dumps of Relative80 and Direct10 (`scripts/build_scale_tables.py --direct`), for the training of its model set and for the outlook models.
